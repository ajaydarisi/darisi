# DARISI User Research Plan

## Status

This is a content-led research synthesis, not a substitute for talking to
prospective clients. It is based on the work, capability areas, blog topics,
and contact flow already present on darisi.in.

## Research objective

Validate that visitors understand who Ajay helps, can find credible proof for
their situation, and know what to do next without mistaking the site for a
generic developer résumé.

## Primary audiences

| Audience | Trigger | What they need to learn quickly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Growing-business founder or operations lead | Spreadsheets, brittle automation, reporting gaps, or a workflow that has outgrown a tool | Whether an internal system is worth building and whether Ajay understands operational complexity |
| Product founder or technical lead | A role-aware product, marketplace, portal, or platform layer needs shipping | Whether design and implementation can stay connected through delivery |
| Commerce operator | A trustworthy, localised, payment-aware purchasing or rental flow is difficult | Evidence of high-trust commerce, regional language, and payment work |
| Global product-team lead | Remote execution needs clear written decisions and visible progress | Whether collaboration will be reliable across time zones |
| Recruiter or peer | Technical and product credibility check | Shipped work and a clear professional point of view |

## Jobs to be done

- Help me decide whether custom software is warranted, then scope the smallest
  useful intervention.
- Turn an ambiguous product idea into a usable, role-aware web application.
- Make an operational, payment, auth, or multilingual flow clear and dependable.
- Assess whether working remotely with Ajay will be low-friction and reliable.

## Current journey

```text
Referral, search, or live-product visit
  → homepage or relevant article
  → recognised problem and capability
  → evidence: case study / practical writing
  → confidence in clarity and delivery
  → email conversation
```

## Design implications to validate

1. The evidence ledger should make ownership and outcome easier to scan than a
   generic portfolio card.
2. The contact prompt should reduce blank-page anxiety without asking visitors
   to qualify themselves.
3. The visual system should be read as calm and rigorous, not merely dark and
   technical.
4. Product, internal-tool, commerce, and async delivery visitors should all
   recognise a relevant path before the first major scroll.

## Lean study

**Method:** six remote, moderated 45-minute sessions over one week. Combine a
context interview with a homepage and article-to-contact usability test.

**Recruit:**

- Two founders or operations leads with a real workflow problem.
- Two product founders or technical leads planning a web product.
- One commerce or multilingual-business operator.
- One US/Europe-based team lead who has worked with remote specialists.

## Session guide

1. **Warm-up — 5 min:** “Tell me about the last workflow or product build you
   needed help with.”
2. **Context — 10 min:** Existing tools, decision makers, risks, and what
   triggered the search for help.
3. **Homepage test — 12 min:** Five-second impression; then ask participants
   to explain the offer, find relevant proof, and identify their next step.
4. **Article-to-contact test — 10 min:** Start from a post closest to their
   situation. Ask what advice they would take, what proves expertise, and what
   they expect to happen after reaching out.
5. **System reaction — 5 min:** Test hierarchy, the evidence ledger, and
   contact prompt language.
6. **Wrap-up — 3 min:** Ask what felt unclear or what proof was missing.

## Success criteria

- At least four of six participants accurately describe the offer and a
  relevant use case without prompting.
- At least four find convincing proof and a next step within two minutes.
- No more than one participant reads the site primarily as a job-seeking résumé.
- Participants can describe what happens after contact; if not, revise the
  contact pattern before adding more visual polish.

## Measurement follow-up

Retain current CTA and outbound-project events. Add article CTA or contact
intent events only after research shows which visitor journeys matter most;
email clicks alone are not a proxy for a qualified conversation.
